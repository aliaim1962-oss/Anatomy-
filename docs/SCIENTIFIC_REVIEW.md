1. Record the exact textbook title, edition, publication details, and the verified chapter/page or other precise source location.
2. Have a qualified anatomy/rehabilitation reviewer check the explanation and its English/Russian equivalence.
3. Separate qualitative teaching descriptions from empirical measurements and clinical prescriptions.
4. Distinguish open/closed chain, loaded/unloaded, and active/passive motion where relevant.
5. Document the provenance, units, assumptions, and limitations of any numerical motion data or anatomical asset.
6. Keep geometry, motion validation, and clinical-content verification as separate statuses; a correct UI or passing software test establishes none of these clinical statuses.
